## Antenatal appointments in the third trimester

- Antenatal care is the care you get from health professionals during pregnancy
  to help keep you and your baby as healthy as possible; you will usually see a
  midwife, and also a doctor if you need to — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- You will be offered at least 10 antenatal appointments if this is your first
  baby, and at least 7 appointments if you have given birth before — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- At 31 weeks, if this is your first baby, the midwife discusses preparing for
  the birth, your preferences for birth, and knowing when you are in labour — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- The 34 week appointment includes a blood pressure check, a urine test, a bump
  measurement, a discussion of your baby's movements, and a mental health check — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- At the 36 week appointment the midwife feels your bump to check the position
  of the baby, measures your bump, and discusses preparing for the birth,
  your preferences for birth, and knowing when you are in labour — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- At the 38 and 40 week appointments the midwife feels your bump to check the
  baby's position and discusses your choices if you are still pregnant after 40
  weeks — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- You can contact maternity services at any time during your pregnancy if you
  are worried about your health or your baby's health, and you do not have to
  wait until your next appointment — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- The 41 week appointment is offered if you are still pregnant after 41 weeks,
  and the midwife discusses your choices with you — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Group B strep screening

- Group B strep is a type of streptococcal bacteria, is very common in both men
  and women, usually lives in the bottom (rectum) or vagina, and is normally
  harmless so most people do not realise they have it — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Group B strep is usually only a problem if you are pregnant and it could
  spread to your baby, in young babies where it can make them very ill, or if you
  are older or already very ill — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Group B strep is not routinely tested for, but may be found during tests
  carried out for another reason, such as a urine test or vaginal swab — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Routine testing is not currently recommended and tests are rarely done on the
  NHS, because group B strep is very common and testing cannot predict whether a
  baby will get an infection; you can pay for a test privately — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- If tests find group B strep while you are pregnant, or you have had a baby
  affected by it before, you may be advised to contact your midwife as soon as
  you go into labour or your waters break, and to have antibiotics into a vein
  during labour, which can significantly reduce the risk of your baby getting
  ill — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- You may also be advised to stay in hospital for at least 12 hours after giving
  birth so your baby can be monitored, and to speak to your midwife about your
  birth plan as they may recommend giving birth in hospital — https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/group-b-strep/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Fetal movement awareness

- You should feel your baby move right up to and during labour — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- There is no set number of movements you should feel each day, every baby is
  different, and you do not need to count the number of kicks or movements you
  feel each day; the important thing is to get to know your baby's usual pattern
  of movements from day to day — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Call your midwife or maternity unit immediately if your baby is moving less
  than usual, you cannot feel your baby moving anymore, or there is a change to
  your baby's usual pattern of movements; they will need to check your baby's
  movements and heartbeat, and you should not wait until the next day — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- If your baby is not well they may be less active than usual, and less movement
  can be a sign of a problem; the sooner this is found out the better, so you and
  your baby can be given the right treatment and care — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Do not use a home doppler (heartbeat listening kit) to try to check the baby's
  heartbeat yourself, as this is not a reliable way to check your baby's health
  and hearing a heartbeat does not mean your baby is well — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Signs of preterm labour

- Premature labour is labour that happens before the 37th week of pregnancy, and
  about 8 out of 100 babies will be born prematurely — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Call your midwife or maternity unit if you are less than 37 weeks pregnant and
  you have regular contractions or tightenings, period-type pains, a gush or
  trickle of fluid from your vagina that could be your waters breaking, or
  backache that is not usual for you — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- You can also call your midwife or maternity unit if you are unsure or worried
  about anything — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- The midwife or hospital will offer checks, tests and monitoring to find out
  whether your waters have broken, whether you are in labour, or whether you have
  an infection; these may include a vaginal examination, blood test, urine test
  and cardiotocography to record contractions and the baby's heartbeat — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- If your waters have broken before 37 weeks (preterm pre-labour rupture of
  membranes), there is an increased risk of infection for you and your baby and
  you will be offered antibiotics and tests for infection — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/premature-labour-and-birth/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Preparing for birth and the birth plan

- A birth plan is a way of letting your midwife, nurses and doctors know what
  you want to happen during your labour, and is a chance to plan things like
  where you want to give birth, who is going to be with you during the birth, and
  what facilities you would like to use — https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/what-to-include-in-your-birth-plan/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Things to think about include where you want to give birth (at home, in a
  midwifery unit or in hospital), who you want with you, birthing equipment such
  as mats or beanbags, special facilities such as a birthing pool, staying active
  and your preferred position, immediate skin-to-skin contact before the cord is
  cut, who may be present including staff in training, pain relief preferences,
  how you want to feed your baby, and vitamin K for your baby — https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/what-to-include-in-your-birth-plan/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Giving birth does not always go perfectly to plan and things may have to
  change at the last minute; your midwife will be there to help you make the
  best and safest decision for you and your baby — https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/what-to-include-in-your-birth-plan/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Signs that labour has begun

- Signs that labour might be starting include contractions or tightenings, a
  show when the plug of mucus from your cervix comes away, backache, an urge to
  go to the toilet caused by the baby's head pressing on your bowel, and your
  waters breaking — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Call your midwife or maternity unit for guidance if you think you are in
  labour, or if you are having regular contractions coming every 5 minutes or
  more often — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Call urgently if your waters break, you have vaginal bleeding, your baby is
  moving less than usual, you are less than 37 weeks pregnant and think you might
  be in labour, any contraction lasts longer than 2 minutes, or you are having 6
  or more contractions every 10 minutes; call 999 and ask for an ambulance if you
  think your baby is coming now and you have a strong urge to push — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ — fetched 22 October 2026
- Braxton Hicks contractions are practice contractions, may feel uncomfortable
  but are usually painless, generally do not last that long, do not happen very
  frequently and do not build up; your contractions tend to become longer,
  stronger and more frequent as your labour progresses — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/signs-that-labour-has-begun/ — fetched 22 October 2026

## Group B strep: US guidance (added during parent review)

- ACOG and the CDC recommend screening all pregnant women for group B strep with
  a swab of the vagina and rectum at 36 to 37 weeks of each pregnancy, and giving
  antibiotics during labor to those who test positive; screening is recommended
  even when a caesarean birth is planned — https://www.cdc.gov/group-b-strep/testing/index.html — fetched 22 October 2026
