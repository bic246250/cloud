Fetched 2026-10-22.

- NHS says you do not need to "eat for 2" even if expecting twins or triplets, and points to the Eatwell Guide for weekly balance - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/have-a-healthy-diet/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- SACN: in the last 3 months of pregnancy women need around an extra 200 kcal a day; exclusive breastfeeding needs around an extra 330 kcal a day for the first 6 months; intentional weight loss in pregnancy is not recommended; there are currently no UK guidelines for appropriate gestational weight gain - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/nutrition-and-maternal-weight-outcomes-sacn-report/nutrition-and-maternal-weight-outcomes-sacn-report-summary - fetched 2026-10-22
- ACOG Committee Opinion 548 (January 2013) adopts and reaffirms the IOM 2009 ranges, counsels on benefits of appropriate weight gain, and records criticism that targets are too high and do not separate degrees of obesity - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2013/01/weight-gain-during-pregnancy - fetched 2026-10-22
- NICE NG247 (published and last reviewed 15 January 2025) sets no total weight gain target, gives no detailed healthy diet advice, refers pre/post-pregnancy weight management to NG246, and recommendation 1.2.14 lists risks of too much and too little gain - https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng247 - fetched 2026-10-22
- WHO Technical Advisory Group on Gestational Weight Gain (TAG-GWG) established May 2023 to advise on developing global GWG standards and optimal ranges, described as a critical gap - https://www.who.int/groups/technical-advisory-group-on-gestational-weight-gain-(tag-gwg) - fetched 2026-10-22
- Critique: the 2009 IOM guidelines varied only minimally from the 1990 guidelines, the 1990 recommendations rested on 1980s data, and many critics advocate lower targets, especially for women with obesity - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4989622/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- IOM committee states its guidelines are intended for use among women in the United States and may apply in other developed countries, but are not intended where women are substantially shorter or thinner - https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12584/chapter/9 - fetched 2026-10-22
- Critique: estimates of weight at conception and total GWG vary by method, and small differences can move a woman into a different BMI category and a different recommendation - https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/oby.20951 - fetched 2026-10-22
