## Sources

- The EDD is 280 days after the first day of the last menstrual period by convention; this assumes a regular 28-day cycle with ovulation on day 14 and does not account for poor LMP recall, irregular cycle length, or variation in ovulation timing - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- ACOG reports that approximately one half of women accurately recall their LMP - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- In one study 40% of women who had first-trimester ultrasonography had their EDD adjusted because of a discrepancy of more than 5 days; EDDs were adjusted in only 10% of a control group scanned in the second trimester - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- Naegele's rule has been in use for about 200 years and assumes a very regular 28-day cycle with correct recall of the first day of the last period - https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/r0fcsaml/baby-due-dates-and-how-it-affects-my-care.pdf - fetched 2026-10-22
- If the patient is unsure of her LMP, dating should be based on ultrasound examination estimates, ideally obtained before or at 13 6/7 weeks of gestation - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- Ultrasound measurement of the embryo or fetus in the first trimester (up to and including 13 6/7 weeks) is the most accurate method to establish or confirm gestational age; crown-rump length dating has an accuracy of plus or minus 5 to 7 days and is more accurate the earlier in the first trimester it is performed - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- Re-dating cutoffs: more than 7 days before 14 0/7 weeks; more than 5 days before 9 0/7 weeks; more than 7 days at 14 0/7 to 15 6/7 weeks; more than 10 days at 16 0/7 to 21 6/7 weeks; more than 14 days at 22 0/7 to 27 6/7 weeks; more than 21 days in the third trimester - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- Second-trimester ultrasound dating accuracy is plus or minus 7 to 10 days between 14 0/7 and 21 6/7 weeks and plus or minus 10 to 14 days between 22 0/7 and 27 6/7 weeks; third-trimester dating is the least reliable at plus or minus 21 to 30 days - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- A pregnancy without an ultrasound examination that confirms or revises the EDD before 22 0/7 weeks of gestational age should be considered suboptimally dated - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- If pregnancy resulted from assisted reproductive technology the ART-derived gestational age should be used; for IVF the EDD is assigned using the age of the embryo and the date of transfer, with 261 days from embryo replacement for a day-5 embryo and 263 days for a day-3 embryo - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date - fetched 2026-10-22
- Pregnancy normally lasts from 37 weeks to 42 weeks from the first day of the last period; the first midwife appointment is offered at 8 to 12 weeks and asks for the date of the last period - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/finding-out/due-date-calculator/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- The 40 week and 41 week antenatal appointments discuss choices if you are still pregnant after 40 weeks and after 41 weeks - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- Only about 1 in 20 babies arrive on their due date - https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/hospital-bag-checklist/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- The due date is an estimate of when the pregnancy reaches 40 weeks and it is common to give birth before or after the due date; there are no proven ways to bring on labour safely at home - https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/preparing-for-labour-and-birth/overdue-have-you-gone-past-your-due-date/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- The baby can be born any time between 37 and 42 weeks, although most babies are born by 41 weeks; only 4.4% of babies come on their due date - https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/r0fcsaml/baby-due-dates-and-how-it-affects-my-care.pdf - fetched 2026-10-22
- It is usually safe to go up to a week past the due date (from 40 to 41 weeks); once you reach 41 weeks the risks start to increase, including caesarean birth, neonatal unit care, stillbirth and neonatal death; around 1 in 5 births are induced - https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-41/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- Induction is offered if you do not go into labour naturally by 41 weeks because the risk of stillbirth or problems for the baby is then higher; a membrane sweep is usually offered after 39 weeks; if the pregnancy lasts longer than 42 weeks and you decline induction, increased monitoring should be offered - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/labour-and-birth/inducing-labour/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- Term classification: early term 37 0/7 to 38 6/7 weeks; full term 39 0/7 to 40 6/7 weeks; late term 41 0/7 to 41 6/7 weeks; postterm 42 0/7 weeks and beyond - https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2013/11/definition-of-term-pregnancy - fetched 2026-10-22
