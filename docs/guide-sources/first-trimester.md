## Dating and the weeks 1-4 section

- Pregnancy is dated from the first day of the last period, and the booking
  appointment is when the midwife asks for that date — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- NHS week-by-week guidance splits pregnancy into a first trimester covering
  weeks 4 to 12 — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Weeks 5-8 symptoms

- NHS lists tiredness, vomiting and morning sickness, back pain, headaches,
  indigestion and heartburn, pelvic pain, piles, stretch marks and vaginal
  discharge among common pregnancy symptoms — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Nausea and vomiting in pregnancy is very common in early pregnancy and can
  affect you at any time of the day or night — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Weeks 9-13

- By week 12 the baby is about the size of a plum, the waist thickens and
  sickness eases as the first trimester closes — https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-12/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Screening

- Combined test: blood sample between 10 and 14 weeks plus ultrasound at the
  dating scan between 11 and 14 weeks; quadruple test offered between 14 and 20
  weeks; results give a chance, not a diagnosis; screening is a choice — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/screening-for-downs-edwards-pataus-syndrome/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Symptoms and what helps

- Morning sickness usually clears up by weeks 16 to 20; hyperemesis gravidarum
  can cause dehydration; NHS self-care advice (rest, dry toast, small frequent
  low-fat meals, sipping fluids, ginger, acupressure); when to call a midwife,
  GP or 111 — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Feeling tired is common in pregnancy, especially in the first 12 weeks, and
  NHS advice is to rest as much as possible — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/tiredness/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Severe headache can be a symptom of pre-eclampsia; high temperature, fluid
  leaking from the vagina, itchy skin, sudden breathlessness, pain and mental
  health changes all need checking — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pregnancy-symptoms-you-need-to-get-help-for/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Appointments and screening timeline

- At least 10 antenatal appointments and 2 ultrasound scans for a first baby,
  at least 7 if you have given birth before; booking appointment at 8 to 12
  weeks; dating scan at 11 to 14 weeks; 16 week appointment; 18 to 21 week
  scan; maternity services can be contacted at any time — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Screening timing: sickle cell and thalassaemia usually before 10 weeks,
  hepatitis B, HIV and syphilis usually between 8 and 12 weeks, combined test
  usually between 11 and 14 weeks, quadruple test between 14 and 20 weeks,
  20-week scan between 18 and 21 weeks — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/screening-tests/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Foods to avoid and food safety

- Unpasteurised milk, cream, yoghurt, butter and ice cream should be avoided;
  mould-ripened soft cheeses with a white rind and soft blue-veined cheeses must
  be cooked until steaming hot; raw or undercooked meat carries a small risk of
  toxoplasmosis; liver and liver products are high in vitamin A; hen eggs with
  the British Lion stamp can be eaten raw or runny, others should be well
  cooked; avoid shark, swordfish and marlin; limit tuna to 4 medium cans or 2
  steaks and oily fish to 2 portions a week; listeriosis can cause miscarriage
  or stillbirth; if you have already eaten something to avoid and did not
  become ill it is unlikely to affect your baby — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Fruit, vegetables and salads should be washed to remove soil, which can
  contain toxoplasma — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/have-a-healthy-diet/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- The recommendation is not to drink alcohol at all in pregnancy; drinking
  increases the risk of miscarriage, premature birth and low birthweight — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/drinking-alcohol-while-pregnant/ — fetched 21 October 2026
- Stopping smoking reduces the risk of stillbirth, premature birth and low
  birth weight, and nicotine replacement therapy can be used in pregnancy — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/stop-smoking/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Folic acid and vitamin D

- 400 micrograms of folic acid daily from before pregnancy until 12 weeks,
  which helps the baby's nervous system form and offers protection from neural
  tube defects such as spina bifida; 5 milligrams daily for those at higher
  chance (including diabetes, anti-epilepsy medicine, anti-retroviral medicine,
  previous affected pregnancy); 10 micrograms of vitamin D daily; do not take
  cod liver oil or supplements containing vitamin A (retinol) — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/pregnancy-vitamins-and-supplements/ — fetched 21 October 2026

## Warning signs that need urgent care

- High temperature of 38C or above, fluid leaking from the vagina, itchy skin
  (possible intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy), severe headache (possible
  pre-eclampsia), sudden or lying-down breathlessness with chest pain or
  dizziness, and change in the baby's movements all need urgent checking — https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pregnancy-symptoms-you-need-to-get-help-for/ — fetched 21 October 2026
