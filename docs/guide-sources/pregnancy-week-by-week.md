## Cited sources

- NHS week-by-week guide groups pregnancy into weeks 4 to 12, 13 to 27, and 28 to 41, and describes the second trimester as about a third of the way through pregnancy - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS advises referring yourself to maternity services as soon as you know you are pregnant, and says you can contact maternity services at any time if you are worried about your health or your baby's health - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: at least 10 antenatal appointments and 2 ultrasound scans for a first baby, at least 7 appointments if you have given birth before - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: first midwife (booking) appointment at 8 to 12 weeks, around 1 hour, including blood pressure and urine checks, blood group test, and questions about health, mental health and home situation - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/your-pregnancy-care/your-antenatal-care-and-appointments/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: nausea and vomiting can occur at any time of day or night, usually clears up by weeks 16 to 20, and does not put the baby at increased risk - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: severe pregnancy sickness (hyperemesis gravidarum) can cause dehydration or malnutrition and needs treatment - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: you should start to feel your baby move between 16 and 24 weeks, later if it is your first baby, and tell your midwife if you have not felt movement by 24 weeks - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: early movements feel like gentle swirling or fluttering; there is no set number of movements to expect and you do not need to count kicks - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: call your midwife or maternity unit immediately if your baby is moving less than usual, you cannot feel movement, or the usual pattern changes - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: do not use a home doppler to check the baby's heartbeat yourself; it is not a reliable way to check the baby's health - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ - fetched 2026-10-22
- NHS: you should feel your baby move right up to and during labour - https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/your-babys-movements/ - fetched 2026-10-22
